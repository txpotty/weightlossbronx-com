    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works for Patients in Harrison, NY

    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works in Harrison

    Standard naltrexone is a 50 mg tablet, FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder, and it blocks opioid receptors for most of the day. Low dose naltrexone drops that to somewhere between 1.5 and 4.5 mg, taken at night. The block lasts a few hours. One leading theory is that this brief interruption nudges the body to raise its own endorphin signaling afterward, a rebound effect researchers are still trying to measure. LDN also seems to quiet microglia, immune cells in the brain and spinal cord linked to pain and inflammation. That is the theory, not settled science.

    Who Low Dose Naltrexone Is For

    Most people who ask about LDN live with fibromyalgia or a long-running inflammatory condition that has not responded well to the usual treatments. Small studies in fibromyalgia and Crohn's disease are the most cited evidence. Some patients also ask about it alongside a weight management plan, though it is not a weight loss drug. Anyone taking opioids is out. The provider wants to know what you hope LDN will change and compares that against what the published studies looked at, which is usually pain and fatigue.

    LDN Dosing and Schedule in Harrison

    A common start is 1.5 mg at bedtime, in a compounded capsule or a liquid you measure with an oral syringe. Every one to two weeks the dose usually steps up by about 1.5 mg until it reaches 3 to 4.5 mg. Night dosing lines the short receptor block up with sleep. Vivid dreams can follow. Patients who sleep badly on it are sometimes moved to a morning dose instead, and plenty of them stay there once it works.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one and two are mostly about sleep. Expect strange, very vivid dreams, along with a few nights of lighter sleep, especially right after the dose goes up. By weeks three and four most patients are near their target dose. The people who benefit tend to notice it slowly, somewhere between 4 and 12 weeks, as fewer flare days or a bit more energy, not as a sudden shift. Your provider checks in during titration. Around month three, you and the provider decide whether it has earned a place in your routine.

    Side Effects of LDN in Harrison

    Vivid dreams and insomnia top the list of side effects in published studies, with headache and mild nausea showing up a little less often. Most of it fades within two weeks of a dose change. The bigger issue is opioids. Because it blocks opioid receptors, LDN can make pain pills like hydrocodone or tramadol useless and can cause withdrawal in regular users. Report dark urine or yellowing skin through secure messaging the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake form asks for every prescription and supplement you take, with a specific question about tramadol and codeine cough syrup because people forget those count as opioids. The provider also asks about your liver. If you have active liver disease or are pregnant, LDN is usually off the table. Nothing is prescribed unless the provider thinks the likely benefit beats the risk in your case. Some patients are asked to upload a recent liver panel showing ALT and AST values.

    How LDN Compares to Related Options in Harrison

    Full-dose naltrexone at 50 mg is FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder and blocks receptors around the clock, which is a different effect from the short nightly block of LDN. Naltrexone gum is another compounded format, newer and with even less published data. On weight loss, GLP-1 drugs win easily. In the STEP trials, semaglutide 2.4 mg averaged roughly 15 percent body weight loss over 68 weeks, and LDN has nothing close to that evidence. Your provider can check whether LDN can sit alongside a GLP-1 or another program you already use.
